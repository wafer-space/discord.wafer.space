[2026-10-23 6:51 p.m.] namibj
the base "muxed e-test" is just an analog switch matrix on the same die as a bunch of DUTs; a simple digital decoder selects one DUT, activates those switches and deactivates the others; there's two wires to each DUT "LEF pin"; off-chip multimeters are then scripted/remote-controlled to sample a few hundred/thousand "interesting" bias conditions of the DUT; then the mux is changed to select the next DUT; then this repeats; after a few minutes the setup rings a bell/buzzer/status-light and a nearby human swaps in the next COB at convenience; this allows measuring hundreds of dies under DC operating point conditions without very expensive raw wafer probe stations.

_Might_ repeat it afterwards with the test chip and it's socket-board in a freezer and/or oven or if thermal mass works out small enough a 2/3 stage peltier chamber might even give the essentially-full specification range of die temperatures without being a genuinely expensive chamber and without the human having to touch each chip once for each temperature.

The big things are just that thin oxide nvt NMOS have zero SPICE and zero e-test data (at least NDA-free), a bunch of stuff has zero local mismatch statistical models, that latter includes (notably!) plain vias which promise a factor of over 3 from typ to max and no min (breaks predictable matching of differential pair resistor divider kind-of structures, without spending a bunch of space and parasticic capacitance on spamming vias in hopes of the unspecified statistics being the type that's persuaded by such tricks).
