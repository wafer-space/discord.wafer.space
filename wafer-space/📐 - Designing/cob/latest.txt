[2026-11-17 11:47 a.m.] anfroholic
Looks like I'm putting some chips on the table regarding wirebonding exchange formats.

Currently have a few problems we need to solve. Right now everything is built around humans reading pictures. And in the age of AI, I think our formats should become more human-centric anyways.

### The premise
A picture is *already* the accepted exchange format today. So keep the picture as an SVG *(which is just XML under the hood)*, and embed wirebonding data directly as key/value pairs in the raw XML.
Proposed format is `myboard.wb.svg`
> Humans get what they want.
> Machines can read what they want.

What we've got so far:
- **Footprint export:** generates 3D models you can add to your board alongside your GDS (see attached). I'm also working on embedding the SVG data directly into the footprint itself.
- **GDS import:** finds the paths in your GDS and uses their names to help you create symbols and nets.
- **Multiple die and stacked die** support.
- **Any bond topology:** board to die, die to die, and everything in between.
- **import/export** of wb.svg files directly
- ...plus a bunch of other fun little tools.

Links and more info:
**App:** https://anfroholic.github.io/wirebonding.svg
**Repo:** https://github.com/anfroholic/wirebonding.svg

Feedback, ideas and issues are all welcome.

Example data:
```xml
<svg xmlns="http://www.w3.org/2000/svg" xmlns:wb="urn:wbx:0.1" viewBox="-300 -500 19000 15600"
     wb:version="0.1" wb:units="um" wb:design="wafer.space 1x1 logo die" wb:rev="A"
     wb:rule-wire-gap="60" wb:rule-edge-clear="40">

  <!-- The board: an outline, and a land per bond finger. 1 unit = 1 µm, so this land is 250 × 1000 µm. -->
  <g>
    <rect x="0" y="0" width="13000" height="13000" class="sub" wb:kind="outline" wb:name="SUB"/>
    <rect x="3175" y="10700" width="250" height="1000" class="cu"
          wb:kind="land" wb:name="L1" wb:net="clk_PAD" wb:finish="ENIG"/>
    <circle cx="900" cy="12100" r="250" class="cu" wb:kind="fiducial" wb:name="F1" wb:role="align" wb:datum="B"/>
  </g>

  <!-- The die, placed by its transform. Inside it, coordinates are the die's own, y up as in its GDS. -->
  <g transform="translate(4534 9061) scale(1 -1)" wb:kind="die" wb:name="U1" wb:gds-cell="chip_top" wb:pad-layer="37/0">
    <rect x="0" y="0" width="3932" height="5122" class="die" wb:kind="outline"/>
    <g transform="scale(1 -1)"><title>U1 artwork</title>
      <image x="0" y="-5122" width="3932" height="5122" href="data:image/png;base64,iVBORw0KGgo…"/>
    </g>
    <rect x="496" y="28" width="60" height="60" class="pad" wb:kind="pad" wb:name="1" wb:net="clk_PAD"/>
  </g>

  <!-- A wire from die pad U1.1 to land L1. stroke-width is the wire's diameter: 20 µm. -->
  <line x1="5060" y1="9003" x2="3300" y2="11000" stroke-width="20" class="w sig"
        wb:kind="wire" wb:name="W01" wb:from="U1.1" wb:to="L1" wb:profile="STD20"
        wb:seq="1" wb:tier="1" wb:net="clk_PAD"/>

  <!-- How the wire loops: a parametric profile that fits any span, drawn beside the plan. -->
  <g wb:kind="profile" wb:name="STD20" wb:material="Au" wb:style="ball-wedge" wb:dir="forward"
     wb:moves="180/0 30/12% -20/35%" wb:interp="spline" wb:ref-span="1000">…</g>

  <!-- Heights: the section view. Each rectangle's height is that layer's thickness: a 280 µm die. -->
  <rect x="4534" y="-305" width="3932" height="280" wb:kind="layer" wb:name="U1" wb:role="die" wb:material="Si"/>

  <!-- Where the die may land: a position tolerance to datums A, B and C. -->
  <g wb:kind="tolerance" wb:applies-to="U1" wb:char="position" wb:tol="50" wb:modifier="CZ" wb:datums="A B C">…</g>
</svg>
```

[2026-11-17 6:11 p.m.] h.tamas
The circular-ish pad layout comes from some optimization pressure where we didn't know the exact design rules so we wanted to make everything as tight as possible to avoid any back-and-forth with the bonding house. I optimized TT02 "by hand" using SolveSpace, and the later iterations use an approximation of this optimum while being easier to generate [semi-automatically](https://github.com/TinyTapeout/cobbler). We also happen to like how it looks, but that's a secondary concern.

There are other points where I have issues with our current setup. It's not as automated as I'd like, and as you mention, the fanout wires would logically belong to the pcb level. I agree with that in principle, but when I actually tried, it became a nuisance to wire up the pads because the space is too tight for horizontal, vertical and 45° wires. Hence the workaround to include the fanout in the generated footprint and to designate them as irregularly shaped pads. (I have a design boundary where the pcb is manually edited and the footprint is mostly generated, so that changing some settings and regenerating the footprint wouldn't overwrite any of my manual work.)

Anyway, if all the bonding information is going to be embedded in the kicad footprint file and I can generate it in a scripted way and also export the diagrams, models and any other data for the foundry in a scripted way, then I think we should be fine (pending the non-axis-aligned landing pads).

And thanks for pushing for better COB support.

It will depend on what you want to get and lots of luck. Second hand wire bonders are such an **illiquide market **that getting a consistent pricing is really a challenge. 

Auction seems to have the best prices, but are also the most variable. 
Ebay listing are more predictable and expect prices to start at least around 1.5k USD. 
 
Around my area (US) you are looking at about 300$ or lower (have seen 2xwire bonders + tester go for as low as 120$) for a good second hand wire bonder at auction, but you also need to account for shipping, which will actually be the bulk of your cost. How much do you value the 18h drive and is your trunk big though ? 

For reference: Fully automated bonding machines go for 3k USD but are also quite rare. (So more expensive then electron microscopes)

[2026-11-21 3:37 a.m.] thatreprapguy
Couple of approaches: (1) Dipping the probe and using it as a pen. This produces dots 20-4μm diameter and you get about 100 dots before you need to re-dip the probe (depends on viscosity, exact probe dimensions, phase of moon etc.) (2) Use a negative photoresist and a UV head. This doesn't need a thin film like chip production does (though it can work with one) because it only sets the resist between the probe tip and the substrate. The height of the probe determines the thickness of the solidified resist. This can get down to 4-5μm and is considerably faster than (1).
