Ohhhhh... Yeah that's quite a lot.

It'll depend heavily on how flexible your memory accesses need to be for what your chip is trying to be good at.

I'd suggest to potentially wait until our multi-gigabit transceiver works have progressed some; at least as far as the parts of the compute with such low compute intensity go; there should be some lower speed variants.
For example, if 8 Gbit/s raw data rate (PCIe 3.0) works out decently for duplex communications, literally state of the art CXL memory would work with it (at, for it, reduced speed; apparently it also doesn't like less than 4 lanes without treating it as a degraded state) as they degrade down from 32 GT/s to 16 and 8 GT/s (but not to the older PCIe 1.0/2.0 2.5GT/s / 5 GT/s).

You could also of course use an FPGA as a memory controller / translator from DDR2/3/4 to a suitable custom protocol over a SerDes interconnect to the custom chip.
Honestly, if you're creative enough you could take charge of the IO pad cell/use one of the lower voltage 3rd party ones, and probably manage a full gigabit unidirectional (or half duplex) per data pin, with DDR2/DDR3 signaling.
You just won't get by with the regular SCL synthesis for the faster parts and might not want to aim at slow corner PVT chips doing particularly high speeds. If the application allows do consider aiming for the typical process corner under non-bad voltage and not too high temperature to slightly exceed the speeds the rest of the circuit would realistically support...

[2026-09-16 11:08 p.m.] namibj
Also remember DDR2 (e.g.) RAM is a bus thing; you're not prevented from addressing an IO controller over the bus if that IO controller plays by the rules; you're also allowed to hand over responsibility of refresh management and segment off drive of the command/address bus of such memory to a different chip temporarily.
Though I'd probably not suggest splitting off in a way that forces you to use some external MUX crossbar chip to dynamically route memory bus data wires between different "memory hosts".

Memory "mapped" IO is probably the most realistic way, though if you own the memory controller logic you'll be VERY free to do things differently for the protocol as long as it fits with the actual memory access pattern structure and the normal memory chips don't get confused from what you're doing to them.
